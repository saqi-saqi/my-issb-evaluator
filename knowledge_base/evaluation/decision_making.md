# EVALUATION RUBRIC: DECISION-MAKING & PROBLEM SOLVING
Source Type: Evaluation Rubric
Dimension: Deputy President & Psychologist
Category: Decision Making

## Definition
The cognitive capability to evaluate critical situations systematically, recognize ethical constraints, prioritize competing demands, and commit to sound courses of action under uncertainty or pressure.

## Observable Positive Indicators
- Systematic Reasoning: Explains the thought process behind a decision ("First, I evaluated the safety risk; second, I prioritized securing communications...").
- Consideration of Trade-Offs: Acknowledges that difficult choices often involve sacrifices or risks, demonstrating mature realism.
- Adherence to Core Ethics: Upholds integrity, duty, and human dignity even when a shortcut or compromise would be easier.
- Cognitive Flexibility: Expresses willingness to adjust tactics if initial information proves erroneous or ground realities change.
- Practical Realism: Proposes feasible, actionable solutions grounded in available resources and practical common sense.

## Observable Potential Weaknesses
- Impulsive Reaction: Reaches conclusions without evaluating immediate consequences or safety implications.
- Paralysis / Evasion: Hesitates to choose between difficult options, repeatedly seeking external validation or avoiding making a choice.
- Ethical Compromise: Readily abandons principles, truth, or peer welfare for personal gain or self-preservation.
- Dogmatic Stubbornness: Refuses to re-evaluate flawed choices despite clear evidence of error.

## Methodological Guardrail
In situational dilemma scenarios (e.g. ethical puzzles or tactical choices), evaluate the rigor and moral integrity of the candidate's reasoning process rather than requiring one dogmatic "correct" answer.
