# EVALUATION RUBRIC: INTEGRITY & SENSE OF RESPONSIBILITY
Source Type: Evaluation Rubric
Dimension: Deputy President & Psychologist
Category: Integrity, Moral Courage & Accountability

## Definition
The moral courage to adhere to truth, accept personal accountability for actions and failures, uphold institutional honor under pressure, and faithfully discharge duties without supervision or coercion.

## Observable Positive Indicators
- Transparent Accountability: Readily acknowledges personal mistakes without hedging, blaming subordinates, or excusing failures.
- Moral Courage: Demonstrates willingness to uphold ethical standards even when it is uncomfortable or unpopular among peers.
- Fidelity to Truth: Answers questions with candor and intellectual honesty, gracefully admitting knowledge limitations ("I do not know, Sir") rather than fabricating facts.
- Institutional Respect: Understands and respects constitutional, legal, and military norms of duty, service, and sacrifice.
- Conscientiousness: Shows consistent dedication to assigned tasks, punctuality, and pride in doing quality work regardless of recognition.

## Observable Potential Weaknesses
- Evasion of Responsibility: Blames circumstances, parents, teachers, or teammates for personal failures.
- Intellectual Dishonesty: Attempts to bluff, guess wildly, or fabricate answers when confronted with unknown facts.
- Opportunism / Expediency: Readily compromises principles or rules for personal comfort, status, or superficial advantage.
- Defensiveness Under Scrutiny: Becomes agitated, resentful, or combative when probed about inconsistencies or personal weaknesses.
- Terse / Dismissive Attitude: Utters dismissive "yes" or "no" when asked to reflect on moral choices or personal conduct.

## Methodological Guardrail
A candidate who candidly admits an error or past misjudgment demonstrates higher moral caliber than one who constructs elaborate, flawless self-justifications. Honest vulnerability is an indicator of integrity, not weakness.
