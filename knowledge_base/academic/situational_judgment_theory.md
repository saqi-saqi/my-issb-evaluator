# Academic Research: Situational Judgment and Structured Interview Reliability
Source Type: Academic (Level 2)
Title: Predictive Validity and Fairness of Situational Judgment Tests and Structured Interviews
Key References: McDaniel et al. (2007) Situational Judgment Tests; Campion et al. (1997) A Review of Structured Interviews; Lievens & Sackett (2012) The Validity of SJTs in Selection.

## Methodological Validity Principles

### 1. Superiority of Behavioral & Situational Probing
- Structured past-behavior questions ("Describe a time when you...") and situational scenario questions ("What would you do if...") possess significantly higher criterion-related validity than unstructured, impressionistic interviews (Campion et al., 1997).

### 2. Standardization and Multi-Rater Reliability
- Assessing candidates against anchored behavioral rubrics minimizes cognitive biases (halo effect, primacy/recency bias, demographic stereotyping).
- Evaluating multiple data points across independent domains yields a balanced holistic judgment.

### 3. Observable Indicators vs Trait Attribution Guardrail
- Inferring stable, long-term personality traits (e.g. "Candidate is inherently dishonest" or "Candidate has poor emotional stability") from a single conversational exchange violates fundamental psychometric principles.
- Valid assessments restrict evaluation to observed behavioral evidence during the specific interaction, noting both demonstrated strengths and observed growth opportunities.
